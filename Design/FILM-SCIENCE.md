# Film science

How Mira renders a film look, why each number is what it is, and how to add a film. This is the reference for `Recipe.pipeline` 2 (every recipe that has chosen a film). The code is in `Packages/MiraCore/Sources/MiraCore` (`Renderer.swift`, `FilmCharacter.swift`, `FilmMath.swift`, `GradeKernels.swift`, `Reciprocity.swift`, `ProceduralLooks.swift`, `PackedLUT.swift`) and the Core Image kernels in `Packages/MiraCore/Kernels/Grade.ci.metal`. Nothing here is a measurement of a particular stock: the colour of a stock comes from its LUT, and everything else is a model built from published behavior, with the assumptions marked.

## 1. Pipeline 2, stage by stage

The order follows the light: what the film is exposed to, what the film does, then what printing, scanning and finishing do to the result. The shared `CIContext` works in extended linear sRGB with half-float intermediates. "Linear" below means that working space; "encoded" means sRGB-encoded values. Every spatial parameter is relative to the frame (`scale = min(width, height) / 1000`, or a fraction of the diagonal), so a preview and a full-size export look the same.

1. **Crop.** Crop, 90-degree rotation, flips and straighten, first, so every later stage and every frame-relative size sees the cropped picture.
2. **Exposure, push and scene-referred headroom** (linear; kernel `miraFilmExposure`, scalar mirror `FilmMath.exposure`). The gain is `2^(exposure + 0.33 * push)`: a push develops for more density, so it also brightens. For an SDR source (headroom 1) a gain g above 1 rolls highlights off like a negative's shoulder, `x (g + x) / (1 + g x)`, blended with the plain gain by the film's latitude (0 plain gain, 1 full shoulder); a gain of 1 or less is a plain gain. Slide and Kodachrome lose latitude when pushed (`latitude *= max(0, 1 - 0.3 * push)`). A scene-referred source (RAW developed with extended range, HDR) carries a measured headroom H > 1 (up to 8): when the white point W = g H exceeds 1, values above a knee at 0.6 go through `y = k + (1 - k) t (1 + a) / (1 + a t)`, with `t = (g x - k) / (W - k)` and `a = (W - 1) / (1 - k)`, so W maps to exactly 1, the curve is monotone and concave, and its slope is 1 at the knee. The result is blended with the plain gain by latitude. Highlights the sensor held are therefore compressed by the film instead of clipped at the LUT.
3. **White balance** (linear). `CITemperatureAndTint` with the neutral at 6500 K and the target moved by warmth (up to 1500 K) and tint (up to 40): the light the film is exposed to, so before the film.
4. **Reciprocity** (linear; kernel `miraReciprocity`, `FilmMath.reciprocity`). The effect of the exposure time on the film (section 4): a per-channel gain `2^(darkening + cast)` (darkening is the lost stops, only in Uncompensated mode), then a power around 18% grey, `0.18 (x / 0.18)^contrast`. Skipped when the mode is Off, no time is known, or the time has no effect (any time up to the film's no-correction limit).
5. **Dodge and burn** (painted light). Before the colour and film stages, so the film responds to it like a print made from a dodged and burned negative; the strokes carry the grain with them.
6. **Colour mixer, then curves.** The photographer's own corrections, before the film so the film sits on top of them.
7. **Halation** (linear; `miraHighlights`, two Gaussian blurs, `miraHalationCombine`). The highlight part of the picture, `p * smoothstep(0.7, 1.0, luma(p))`, is blurred at two radii and put back tinted: `out = src - amount * c * h + amount * tint/luma(tint) * luma(blur(h))`, with partial compensation `c = 0.2`. The glow is 0.65 of a Gaussian at sigma plus 0.35 at sigma / 3, with `sigma = halationRadius * diagonal * 2^((radiusSlider - 0.5) * 2)` (radius 0.006 of the diagonal at the slider's middle). `amount = Halation slider * halationStrength`, clamped to 0.9; rem-jet stocks multiply it by 0.15 (backing intact) or by 2.5 with a red tint `[1.0, 0.15, 0.03]` when the rem-jet is removed.
8. **Film LUT** (`miraLUT3D`). The cube is rearranged into a float atlas (width dim x dim, height dim, nearest sampling, cached per film id), the working-space pixel is encoded to sRGB and clamped, interpolated tetrahedrally in float, and decoded again. The result is blended with the pre-LUT image by Strength.
9. **Paper or Scan, then push contrast and black lift.** Paper (a Mira original print cube: `cinema-print` or `color-paper`, `ProceduralLooks`) is applied with the same float kernel and blended by Strength; with a paper set the scan profile is ignored. Without one, the Scan profile (`miraScan`, encoded) applies: colour shift, contrast, saturation, black lift, highlight shoulder. Push adds contrast (`pushContrastPerStop * push`) and, for color negative, motion picture and black-and-white stocks, a black lift of `0.012 * push` (pushed negatives have fog). Paper is ignored for monochrome films (section 2.3).
10. **Toning** (`miraToning`, monochrome films only). Luminance-preserving split toning of the encoded image.
11. **Contrast, tone, texture, clarity, saturation, vibrance.** The photographer's printing and scanning controls (contrast and saturation on encoded values; Highlights, Shadows, Whites and Blacks through a Metal kernel with masks following a blurred luminance; Texture and Clarity as local contrast).
12. **Bloom** (`CIBloom`, radius `min(100, 25 * scale)`).
13. **Grain** (`miraGrain`, encoded). Smooth value noise (hashed lattice, quintic interpolation, two octaves, scaled to a standard deviation of about 1) in frame-relative coordinates. Amplitude `std = slider * 2 * grainStrength * 0.025 * bandLimit`, where the cell is `grainSize * 2^((sizeSlider - 0.5) * 2)` of the diagonal and `bandLimit = min(1, cell in pixels)`: a pixel that averages f x f grains sees 1/f of the variation, so a downscaled export carries the same visible grain as the preview. Density weighting: `w = 0.25 + 0.75 exp(-((L - center)/0.3)^2)`, faded to zero below L 0.02 to 0.08 and above 0.92 to 0.98. Chroma mixes one shared noise with three per-channel noises (normalized to unit variance). Shadow gain (reciprocity, section 4) multiplies the weight below the tonal centre, 1 at the centre and the full gain at black. Push changes the strength by `1 + pushGrainPerStop * push` (pull: `1 + 0.15 * push`).
14. **Vignette, mask overlay (editor only), border.**

**Pipeline 1** (recipes saved before film science, no `pipeline` key) runs the old order unchanged: exposure, dodge and burn, mixer, curves, film LUT through `CIColorCubeWithColorSpace`, contrast and tone, saturation and vibrance, warmth and tint, a screen-blended halation, bloom, soft-light white-noise grain, vignette, border. A saved edit keeps its pipeline so it reopens the same; choosing a film opts a recipe into pipeline 2.

## 2. Family parameters

`FilmCharacter.defaults(family:iso:monochrome:)` derives a stock's character from its family and ISO. Grain strength is `sqrt(ISO / 400)` times the family factor (clamped 0.3 to 2.5, ISO 400 colour negative is 1.0); grain size is `0.00045 * (ISO / 400)^0.25` of the frame diagonal times the family factor (clamped 0.0002 to 0.0012). A monochrome film (`isMonochrome`: the explicit `monochrome` flag, else the black-and-white family) gets chroma 0, a white halation tint and, outside the B&W family, halation capped at 0.25. Any value can be overridden per film in `films.json` (`"character"`; 16 films do).

| Family | Grain strength x | Grain size x | Grain chroma | Tonal centre | Halation | Latitude | Stops under / over | Rem-jet | Push contrast / stop | Push grain / stop |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| Color Negative | 1.0 | 1.0 | 0.3 | 0.40 | 1.0 | 1.0 | 2 / 3 | no | 0.08 | 0.25 |
| Slide | 0.8 | 1.0 | 0.2 | 0.65 | 0.15 | 0.15 | 2 / 1.5 | no | 0.12 | 0.25 |
| Kodachrome | 0.55 | 1.0 | 0.15 | 0.65 | 0.10 | 0.15 | 1.5 / 1 | no | 0.12 | 0.25 |
| Motion Picture | 1.0 | 1.0 | 0.3 | 0.40 | 1.2 | 1.0 | 3 / 4 | yes | 0.08 | 0.25 |
| Black & White | 1.1 | 1.0 | 0 | 0.40 | 0.25 | 0.9 | 2 / 3 | no | 0.12 | 0.35 |
| Instant | 0.6 | 1.3 | 0.15 | 0.50 | 0.30 | 0.5 | 1 / 1 | no | 0.08 | 0.25 |
| Print | 0.4 | 1.0 | 0.2 | 0.50 | 0.50 | 0.6 | 2 / 2 | no | 0.08 | 0.25 |

All families share the halation radius 0.006 of the diagonal. The halation tint (linear RGB, normalized to unit luminance by the kernel) is `[1.0, 0.28, 0.06]` (red-orange: light reflected off the film base re-exposes the deepest, red-sensitive layer), `[1.0, 0.5, 0.25]` for instant film, and white for monochrome films. The tonal centre is the encoded luminance where grain is most visible: the midtones and shadows of the print for a negative, the light midtones for a slide. Latitude 0 clips like a sensor, 1 is the full negative shoulder; "stops under / over" only feed the latitude readout.

### 2.1 Mira original looks

Four looks are generated in code (`ProceduralLooks.parameters`), evaluated at every lattice point of a 64^3 cube (encoded sRGB in and out) and cached per id. They are Mira originals inspired by the published behavior of cinema negative, cinema print and colour paper; they copy no LUT and measure nothing, and carry no affiliation with Kodak or Fujifilm. Each is a per-channel gain in linear light, a monotone S-curve (linear slope about the pivot 0.5, a tanh toe from `toeStart` into `black`, a tanh shoulder from `shoulderStart` into `white`), saturation about luma with part of the change withheld from skin hues, and split colour balance added in encoded units (weights (1-L)^2 shadows, (1-L)^6 deep shadows, L^2 highlights).

| Look (family) | Balance (linear) | Contrast | Toe start / black | Shoulder start / white | Saturation / skin protection | Tints (shadow, deep shadow, highlight) |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| `vision3-500t` 500T Tungsten (Motion Picture) | 0.90, 1, 1.14 | 0.86 | 0.22 / 0.035 | 0.58 / 0.985 | 0.88 / 0.6 | (-.012, .006, .010), (-.010, .008, .004), (.014, .006, -.012) |
| `vision3-250d` 250D Daylight (Motion Picture) | neutral | 0.98 | 0.20 / 0.028 | 0.64 / 0.975 | 0.95 / 0.5 | (-.006, .003, .006), (-.006, .005, .002), (.010, .004, -.008) |
| `cinema-print` Cinema Print (Print) | neutral | 1.38 | 0.12 / 0.012 | 0.66 / 0.975 | 1.18 / 0.35 | (-.006, 0, .008), (-.004, 0, .006), (.010, .004, -.008) |
| `color-paper` Color Paper (Print) | 1.03, 1, 0.96 | 1.10 | 0.08 / 0.006 | 0.84 / 1.0 | 1.06 / 0.3 | none, none, (.006, .003, -.004) |

The 500T's gains give the cool-blue daylight cast of a tungsten-balanced stock (correct it with Temperature). The two print looks are one implementation behind both their film cards and the Paper stage: a test checks that the Paper stage after an identity film equals the card to within 2/255.

### 2.2 Scan profiles

`ScanParameters` (GradeKernels.swift), our own authored profiles, not measurements of any scanner, in encoded units: Frontier-like is slightly cool (red -0.010, blue +0.015 in the mids, weighted `4 e (1 - e)`), a little more contrast (+0.08) and saturation (+0.06), blacks lifted by 0.015. Noritsu-like is slightly warm (red +0.012, blue -0.012), flatter (contrast -0.06, saturation -0.03) with the highlights softened above 0.85 (softness 0.5, `e -= 0.5 d^2 / 0.3`). Neutral is the unchanged film.

### 2.3 Paper is hidden for monochrome films

A black-and-white negative is printed on black-and-white paper and toned; Toning covers that. The two Paper looks are colour print stocks and tinted B&W films, so the FILMS panel hides the Paper chips when `selectedFilm.isMonochrome`, and the renderer ignores `recipe.paper` for a monochrome film (so a preset that carries a paper cannot tint one; the Scan profile still applies). The chips are also hidden on the Print cards themselves.

## 3. Tuning rationale

These come from visual QA with `mira-sheet --contact-sheet` on `Fixtures/sample.jpg`, judged at 1:1 crops, not on downscaled tiles.

- **Halation.** With full energy conservation (the scattered light taken out of the source in full) halation punched a grey hole in clipped point-light cores. On film the core sits on the shoulder of the characteristic curve, so the removed light is invisible; in a linear render it showed. The fix is partial compensation: only `c = 0.2` of the scattered light is removed. The highlight threshold is `smoothstep(0.7, 1.0)` of luma, the radius 0.006 of the diagonal, and a second Gaussian at sigma / 3 (0.35 of the glow) makes the fringe hug the edge of the light while the wide one (0.65) is the soft tail.
- **Grain.** Judged at 1:1 crops: visible silver grain on Tri-X, near-invisible on Velvia and Kodachrome, as expected from the family factors (Kodachrome has no dye couplers in the emulsion, very fine grain). Band-limiting (step 13) keeps downscaled exports matching previews.
- **Float LUT.** Core Image's colour cube quantized the LUT stage: an identity cube came out with about 257 distinct levels on a 4,000-step ramp. `miraLUT3D` interpolates tetrahedrally in float and gives at least 3,000 levels on the same ramp, with a maximum difference of 1/255 against `CIColorCube` on real colours, at about +30% on that stage. The legacy pipeline keeps `CIColorCubeWithColorSpace` unchanged.
- **Paper and monochrome films.** See 2.3: colour print stocks tinted B&W.
- **Scan profiles and the original looks** are authored (2.1, 2.2); tune them against the contact sheet (`paper-cinema`, `paper-color`, `push-frontier`).

## 4. Exposure time and reciprocity failure

Reciprocity failure is the departure of a film's response from "intensity x time = exposure" at long (and very short) exposures. The research below is condensed from `RECIPROCITY.md` (2026-11-04). "NR" is "not recommended" in the sheet; stops are the sheet's recommended exposure increase (for Fuji and Kodak colour sheets this includes the filter factor). Developer notes are literal sheet text.

### 4.1 What happens

**Long exposures (low-intensity failure, the Schwarzschild effect).** A developable latent-image speck needs several silver atoms on one grain. At low photon flux the early, unstable atoms decay before the next photoelectron arrives, so the same total exposure makes fewer developable grains and effective speed falls. Ilford: "a reduced efficiency in forming stable development centres with lower levels of light" ([Ilford](https://www.ilfordphoto.com/wp/wp-content/uploads/2024/05/Reciprocity-Failure-Compensation-v2.pdf)). Consequences:

- **Colour cast.** Each dye layer has its own failure curve. Kodak: "the sensitivity differences between the many layers of color films can cause a color-balance shift" ([Kodak E-31](https://cyberphoto.se/storage/C0254BB2DFD47BB7DDA9286256ACEF3E997E98C198A401C96283EB306112A972/b21c65bff8d846ad9d061cd1bcbf6adb/pdf/media/c160c5da59fe4ecea361a56f50c24052/Kodak_Reciprocity.pdf)). In the Polaroid colour data the red layer fails most, giving a blue-cyan shift at long times ([Polaroid T-88/669](https://juneleung.github.io/filmdoc/polaroid/88fds.pdf)); every CC filter recommended in the Kodak and Fuji slide and motion-picture tables is red, magenta or yellow-ish, i.e. the filter restores the layers that fail most.
- **Contrast increase.** Failure depends on the light level at the film, so highlights fail less than shadows in the same frame. Ilford: contrast "is increased with long exposures", with pull development ([Ilford](https://www.ilfordphoto.com/wp/wp-content/uploads/2024/05/Reciprocity-Failure-Compensation-v2.pdf)). Kodak's development pulls for classic emulsions (-10%, -20%, -30% at 1, 10, 100 s) encode the same thing ([Kodak F-4017 Tri-X](https://cdn.shopify.com/s/files/1/0339/5113/files/f4017_trix_320400.pdf)).
- **More shadow grain.** Shadows lose more speed, so fewer grains are developed there and they sit lower on the toe. This is an inference from the mechanism, not a datasheet claim.

**High-intensity (short-exposure) failure.** Very high flux creates many small, dispersed latent-image centres instead of a few developable ones. Kodak: "increased scattering of exposed silver halide grains, the formation of smaller latent-image centers, and a lower rate of development", appearing "as lower contrast or reduced density" at 1/1,000 s or shorter; T-MAX, Ektapan and most colour films "have been designed to minimize" it ([Kodak E-31](https://cyberphoto.se/storage/C0254BB2DFD47BB7DDA9286256ACEF3E997E98C198A401C96283EB306112A972/b21c65bff8d846ad9d061cd1bcbf6adb/pdf/media/c160c5da59fe4ecea361a56f50c24052/Kodak_Reciprocity.pdf)).

In the 10-second range, increase exposure 1 stop and use a KODAK WRATTEN 2 Color Compensating Filter CC10R" ([brochure](https://www.kodak.com/content/products-brochures/motion-picture/KODAK-VISION3-500T-5219-7219-brochure.pdf); [tech data](https://www.kodak.com/content/products-brochures/Film/VISION3-500T-Color-Negative-Film-7219-TECHNICAL-DATA.pdf)). VISION3 250D 5207: only "none 1/1000 to 1 s" ([tech data](https://www.kodak.com/content/products-brochures/Film/VISION3-250D-Technical-Data-EN.pdf)). CineStill 800T is Vision3-based, so the 500T row is the assumption for it.

**Instant.** Polaroid 667 (B&W peel-apart): the [data sheet](https://juneleung.github.io/filmdoc/polaroid/667fds.pdf) has only a graph of speed change against time, no loss before about 0.07 s; visual estimates (+-0.2 stop): 1 s -0.5, 10 s -1.3, 100 s -2.4. Polaroid T-88 / 669 (colour peel-apart, [data sheet](https://juneleung.github.io/filmdoc/polaroid/88fds.pdf)): 1/1000 and 1/125 s none, ISO 80; 1/8 s CC30R, ISO 50; 1/4 s CC30R + CC10Y, ISO 40; 1 s CC40R + CC10Y, ISO 25; 10 s CC60R + CC10Y, ISO 10, so stops lost against ISO 80 are 0.68, 1.0, 1.68, 3.0 (the sheet's row after 1/125 s reads "1/3 s, CC10R, ISO 64" as printed, likely 1/30 s, and is left out). The graph shows red failing most, then green, then blue. Fuji FP-100C: the [bulletin](https://issuu.com/dimosd/docs/fp_100c_datasheet/3) is qualitative only. Polaroid 600, SX-70, i-Type, Spectra, Instax, 665: no published numbers.

### 4.3 CC filter conversion

CC filters are rated in density in the dye colour: CCnn = nn/100 density (CC05 = 0.05, CC075 = 0.075; Fuji "2.5G" = 0.025); 0.30 density is one stop, so stops = density / 0.301. Absorbed bands: C absorbs R; M absorbs G; Y absorbs B; R absorbs G and B; G absorbs R and B; B absorbs R and G. Stops lost per channel: CC05R 0.17 on G and B; CC10R 0.33 on G and B; CC05M 0.17 on G; CC10Y 0.33 on B; CC10C 0.33 on R; CC60R + CC10Y 2.0 on G and 2.3 on B. This is first order: real filters have secondary absorptions.

### 4.4 The implemented model (`Reciprocity.swift`)

`Reciprocity.effect(film:exposureTime:)` returns a `ReciprocityEffect` (lost stops, per-channel cast in stops, contrast gain, shadow-grain gain, whether the time is beyond the datasheet). The inputs are the film's datasheet entry (`films.json` `"reciprocity"`, 41 stocks: 34 tables, 7 Schwarzschild rules; variants inherit their parent's) or else the family default, the exposure time (`Recipe.exposureTime`, else the photo's EXIF `ExposureTime`, else none, which turns the stage off) and the mode (`Recipe.reciprocity`).

1. **Loss.** Table: stops are interpolated linearly in log2(time) between the rows, with 0 up to `noCorrectionUpTo` and a ramp from there to the first row (Velvia 50: 0 at 1 s, +1/3 at 4 s). Rows without a number ("not recommended") are skipped. Beyond the last row the last segment is extrapolated in log-time (marked `beyondDatasheet`, shown in the panel as "beyond datasheet"); with a single row the slope is that of Schwarzschild p = 1.2. Schwarzschild: `(p - 1) log2(t / threshold)` stops, threshold 1 s, which for threshold 1 s is Ilford's `t_c = t^p`. Short exposures use the optional `highIntensity` rows `[seconds, stops]`; they lower contrast (`max(0.5, 1 - perStop * loss)`) and cause no cast or grain change.
2. **Cast.** The inverse of the recommended CC filter. With per-channel filter absorption f_c (4.3) and luminance mean `fbar = 0.2126 f_r + 0.7152 f_g + 0.0722 f_b`, the uncompensated extra loss of channel c is `fbar - f_c` stops, applied as an exposure change in linear light before the film, so luminance is preserved. CC10R makes red lose 0.26 stop more and green and blue gain 0.07, so a cyan cast appears, matching Polaroid's statement. Between rows the cast is interpolated per channel in log-time; past the last row it keeps its cast per stop of loss. Films with no filter in the table use the family's `castPerStop`. Monochrome films have no cast.
3. **Contrast.** A power around 18% grey, `0.18 (x / 0.18)^g`, with `g = 1 + contrastPerStop * loss` (0.05 per stop by default; no manufacturer publishes a figure).
4. **Shadow grain.** Gain `min(2, 2^(loss / 2))` on the grain weight below the tonal centre (Poisson counting: noise goes as 1/sqrt(N) and N as 2^(-loss)); a physical estimate, not a datasheet value.
5. **Modes.** *Compensated* (default): the photographer added the tabled stops, so mean exposure is kept but the residue stays (cast, contrast, shadow grain). *Uncompensated*: the picture is also darker by the lost stops. *Off*: the time is ignored. The panel (EXPOSURE TIME slider on a log scale, snapping to standard shutter speeds, double-click for the EXIF time) shows what the time does to this film.
6. **Family defaults** (`Reciprocity.familyDefault`, for stocks without an entry; print has none): Color Negative Schwarzschild p 1.2, threshold 1 s, cast 0 (per-row fits: Fuji X-TRA 400 1.167, Pro 160C 1.183, Pro 400H 1.25, Vision3 500T 1.301; median 1.217; no C-41 sheet prints a CC filter). Black & White p 1.31, threshold 1 s (median of the 11 Ilford exponents; pooling with 8 non-Ilford fitted values gives 1.31 as well; classic-grain stocks fail harder: Tri-X 1.53, APX 100 1.53, Foma 100 1.75, T-MAX 100 1.15, Acros 1.06). Slide: none to 32 s, 64 s +0.04, 128 s +0.45, 240 s +0.75, 480 s +0.83 (median over nine stocks), cast per stop r +0.131, g -0.042, b -0.035. Kodachrome: 0.1 s +1/3 CC05R, 1 s +1/2 CC10Y (merge of K64 and K200), cast per stop r +0.221, g -0.029, b -0.36. Motion Picture: 10 s +1 CC10R, p 1.301 beyond (one datum), cast per stop r +0.261, g -0.071, b -0.071. Instant: none to 0.07 s, 0.25 s +0.60, 1 s +1.09, 10 s +2.15, 100 s +2.4 (mean of 667 and 669), cast per stop r +0.723, g -0.171, b -0.332. The instant and motion-picture cast values are derived from the few filter recommendations that exist.
7. **EXIF.** `ImageSource` reads `ExposureTime` for files and RAW and carries it on the image (`CIImage.miraExposureTime`); a photo without one has no reciprocity effect until a time is chosen.
8. **Strength and what is visible by default.** `Recipe.reciprocityStrength` (0 to 200 %, default 100 %, written only when not 100 %) scales the cast, the contrast residue (`contrastGain - 1`) and the shadow-grain residue (`gain - 1`), and in Uncompensated mode the loss; at 100 % every number is exactly the datasheet model, at 0 % the picture equals Off. It is the user's lever: Mira does not invent a cast or a stronger contrast to make a stock look more dramatic. The defaults stay honest, so a colour-negative stock is subtle in Compensated mode (no published cast, the family `castPerStop` is 0 because no C-41 sheet prints a filter, and a contrast gain of 5 % per stop of loss: Portra 400 at 30 s loses 0.32 stop, so +1.6 % contrast and no cast); slide, Kodachrome, motion-picture and instant stocks show a cast, and Uncompensated shows the darkening of every stock. The readout under the control gives the numbers ("-1 1/3 stops (compensated) - cast G +0.12 st - contrast +7 % - shadow grain +38 %", "beyond datasheet" when extrapolated, "No change at this speed" when nothing is applied).
9. **Pipeline.** Reciprocity, push, scan, paper, toning and rem-jet are pipeline 2 only. A recipe saved before 0.5 (or JSON without a `pipeline` key) is pipeline 1 and ignores them; changing any one of these controls on such a recipe moves it to the current pipeline in the same edit (`Recipe.upgradePipelineIfFilmScienceChanged`, used by the Mac and iOS models), and the FILMS panel says so while the recipe is on pipeline 1. `mira-sheet --recipe-json` without a `pipeline` key uses the current pipeline (note printed) unless `--legacy` is passed. The film-strip thumbnails are rendered with reciprocity Off by design (they are cached per picture and film, not per exposure time).

### 4.5 Deviations and limits

- **Global contrast, not intensity-dependent.** Real failure depends on the light level at each pixel, so shadows fail more than highlights; the ideal is a tone curve in which a pixel L stops from the midtone behaves as if the midtone had been exposed for `t * 2^(-L)`. Mira applies one power around 18% grey and a shadow-grain gain instead. The ideal form was derived (it reproduces Tri-X's -30% development at 100 s as a x1.30 slope) but not implemented.
- **Most sheets give 3 to 6 rows**; interpolation between them is a convenience, not data, and extrapolation beyond the last row is a guess (flagged in the panel). Fuji and Kodak slide stocks have steps of 1/3 stop and CC filters in 2.5-unit increments.
- **Stops are "exposure increase" with filters folded in** for Fuji and Kodak colour tables; treating them as pure luminance loss overstates the dimming slightly.
- **Kodak's current and archived sheets disagree.** Archived E-31 says "none to 10 s" for Portra; the current sheets say "none to 1 s". Mira follows the current sheets for the C-41 family default (threshold 1 s).
- **Data gaps.** Kodachrome 25, Astia, E100G, Rollei, HPS 800, Neopan 1600, Superia (except X-TRA 400), CineStill 50D, Instax, i-Type, 600, SX-70: no manufacturer numbers found, so they use the family default. Instant film rests on two Polaroid stocks, one of them a graph estimate. Independent tests report real failure differing from the sheets, and sheets say "use only as a guide" and "make tests".
- **Not modeled:** temperature, age and lot variation; the Kodak aperture-versus-time ambiguity (Tri-X 100 s: +3 stops or 1200 s); the contrast and shadow-grain terms are derived, not published.

## 5. Packed film payload

The Hald PNGs (64^3 cubes as 512 x 512 images, unmodified from the RawTherapee collection) are provenance only and live in `FilmSources/HaldCLUT/` (361 MB, 260 files, not bundled). The app and the Photos extension bundle `Films/Packed/<id>.mlut` instead (126 MB per copy, 260 files).

**`.mlut` layout** (`PackedLUT`, little endian): the magic `"MLUT"`, UInt16 version (1), UInt16 dimension, UInt8 channels (3), UInt8 transform, UInt8 codec, UInt8 reserved (0), UInt32 raw payload length, then the compressed payload. The payload holds `dimension^3 * 3` UInt16 samples (red fastest, then green, then blue) after a reversible transform (the standard one is a left predictor with the bytes split into planes), compressed with LZMA (LZFSE and zlib are also supported). The container is lossless: decoding gives back exactly the samples that were packed. Decoding takes about 9 to 65 ms.

**Size per film.** `mira-sheet --pack-films FilmSources Films` tries 48^3, 56^3 and the exact 64^3 in that order and keeps the smallest whose error against the 64^3 original, trilinear at the 64^3 lattice points, stays within max 4/255 and 99.9th percentile 1.5/255 per channel (`PackedLUT.acceptedMaxError`, `acceptedP999Error`) on the probe colours: the unique 8-bit colours of `Fixtures/sample.jpg` and of `Fixtures/raw/iphone-xs.dng` (developed with defaults), each scaled to about 512 px on the long side, plus a 64-step neutral ramp and a 36 hue x 8 saturation x 8 value HSV sweep (`PackProbe`). The corners of the cube, mostly saturated and near-clipped colours no photo reaches, are reported for information and do not decide. Current counts: 48^3 for 124 films (27.9 MB), 56^3 for 45 (24.0 MB), exact 64^3 for 91 (73.8 MB): 260 films, 125.6 MB per copy against 361 MB for the PNGs. The app is 284 MB against about 720 MB before. The packer is deterministic, prints a summary table, and replaces `Films/Packed`. Packed films skip the LUT disk cache. `.png` Hald and `.cube` files still load (for user and test libraries); cubes larger than 64 are trilinearly resampled to 64^3.

## 6. Adding a film

1. **Source.** Put the Hald PNG (a level-8 Hald CLUT, 512 x 512, sRGB-encoded, 8 or 16 bit) in `FilmSources/HaldCLUT/<id>.png`.
2. **Catalog entry** in `Films/films.json`: `id` (kebab-case, also the file stem), `displayName`, `brand`, `family` (`colorNegative`, `slide`, `kodachrome`, `blackAndWhite`, `instant`, `motionPicture`, `print`), `iso`, `description` (one sentence), `file` (`Packed/<id>.mlut`), `png` (`HaldCLUT/<id>.png`), `source` (the original path or URL, for attribution). Optional: `parent` (the stock id) and `variant` (a short label such as "+1" or "Cold") for a variant of a stock, which then shows in the Variant picker instead of as a card; `monochrome` (true for a black-and-white instant film, say); `character` (any subset of the fields in section 2 that should differ from the family default); `reciprocity` (a datasheet entry: `{"type": "table", "noCorrectionUpTo": s, "rows": [[seconds, stops|null, {"r":,"g":,"b":}|null]], "highIntensity": [[seconds, stops]], "castPerStop", "contrastPerStop", "source": url}` or `{"type": "schwarzschild", "p": p, "threshold": s, "source": url}`; stops are the sheet's recommended exposure increase, `null` for "not recommended", and the third element is the sheet's CC filter converted to stops per channel as in 4.3; always cite the sheet).
3. **Attribution.** Add the row (id and original file) to `Films/ATTRIBUTION.md`, and keep the licence terms of the source.
4. **Pack:** `swift run -c release --package-path Packages/MiraCore mira-sheet --pack-films FilmSources Films`, then check the summary table and the new file's size.
5. **Tests that guard it:** `FilmCatalogTests` (files exist and ids are unique; the bundled folder has no PNGs; parents and variant labels; every film has an attribution row; every source PNG is referenced once and valid; every packed cube meets the error limits or is exact; character defaults follow ISO and family, and catalog overrides are applied), `PackedLUTTests` (round trips, determinism, corrupt files rejected, a bundled cube renders), `FilmTests` (every bundled film renders plausibly), and `ReciprocityTests` (the Ilford published example and test vectors, table interpolation, cast, no-op at 1/125 s for every film, variants inherit their stock's data). Update any count assertions that the new totals change.
6. **Look at it:** a contact sheet (section 7) at 1:1 crops.

A **procedural look** (a Mira original) has `"file": "procedural:<id>"` and no `png`. Add its `Params` to `ProceduralLooks.parameters` and its id to `ProceduralLooks.ids` (and, for a print look, a `PaperProfile` case and a mapping in `ProceduralLooks.id(for:)`), then a catalog entry as above; no packing is needed, the cube is generated at 64^3 on first use. `ProceduralLooksTests` check that every registered id is cataloged, that the cubes are finite, monotone and plausible, and that the Paper stage equals the card.

## 7. Visual QA

```sh
swift run -c release --package-path Packages/MiraCore mira-sheet --contact-sheet \
  --films Films --input Fixtures/sample.jpg --out build/contact \
  [--films-list id,id,...] [--states a,b,...] [--width 900] [--crop-only] \
  [--exposure-time <seconds>] [--reciprocity off|compensated|uncompensated]
```

It renders a films x states grid headlessly. Default films: `kodak-portra-400, kodak-ektar-100, fuji-velvia-50, kodak-kodachrome-64, kodak-tri-x-400, polaroid-px-680`; unknown ids are skipped with a warning. Default states: `film` (pipeline 2, everything else neutral), `grain` (grainAmount 0.5), `halation` (halationAmount 0.5), `push-frontier` (push +1, Frontier-like scan). Also available: `original`, `halation-max` (halationAmount 1.0, radius 0.5), `paper-cinema`, `paper-color`, `toning-selenium`. `--exposure-time` and `--reciprocity` set `Recipe.exposureTime` and `Recipe.reciprocity` on every tile (a long time and Uncompensated show the cast, contrast and darkening). The input may be a RAW file (it is developed with and without a film as the pipeline does). Output: `<film-id>.png` per film, `contact-sheet.png` (the whole grid), and `crops.png` with 300 x 300 1:1 crops (the brightest compact highlight in the central 60% of the frame, and a midtone region, picked from the source so every film shows the same pixels) in the `grain`, `halation` and `halation-max` states; judge grain and halation there, not on the downscaled tiles. `--crop-only` writes only `crops.png`. Output is deterministic once the LUT disk cache is warm. `scripts/contact-sheet.sh` builds `mira-sheet` in release and renders the standard sheets.
